# Zytrixon V2 — Copywriting & Voice System

**Document:** `16-copywriting.md`  
**Project:** Zytrixon Website V2  
**Status:** Production Copywriting Specification  
**Depends on:** `01-brand-strategy.md`, `02-product-requirements.md`, `14-do-not-do.md`, `15-content-strategy.md`

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines how Zytrixon should sound.

The objective is not to make every sentence “clever.”

The objective is to make Zytrixon sound:

```text
specific
+
intelligent
+
technical
+
business-aware
+
confident
+
human
```

without becoming:

```text
hype-driven
+
corporate
+
generic
+
AI-generated
```

---

# 2. Core Voice

Zytrixon's voice should feel like:

> **An experienced technical partner explaining a complex problem clearly.**

Not:

```text
salesperson
motivational speaker
startup influencer
AI chatbot
corporate press release
```

---

# 3. Voice Attributes

Primary attributes:

```text
Direct
Specific
Calm
Technical
Strategic
Practical
Confident
Human
```

Secondary attributes:

```text
Curious
Precise
Opinionated about engineering quality
Business-aware
```

---

# 4. Confidence Model

Zytrixon should sound confident because it understands the subject.

Confidence should come from:

```text
specificity
clarity
evidence
experience
```

Not from:

```text
superlatives
capital letters
exclamation marks
buzzwords
```

---

# 5. Write Like an Engineer Who Understands Business

Good:

> Your business does not always need another tool. Sometimes it needs the systems you already use to work together.

Bad:

> We leverage cutting-edge digital ecosystems to unlock unprecedented operational synergies.

The first explains something.

The second sounds generated.

---

# 6. Specificity Rule

Prefer:

```text
workflow automation
```

over:

```text
next-generation digital transformation
```

Prefer:

```text
ERP, payment, CRM and internal systems connected through a single workflow
```

over:

```text
seamless digital ecosystem
```

Specific words create credibility.

---

# 7. Sentence Length

Default sentence length:

```text
short to medium
```

Use longer sentences only when explaining a complex relationship.

Avoid paragraphs that contain one enormous sentence.

---

# 8. Paragraph Length

Most website paragraphs should be:

```text
1–4 sentences
```

Dense technical explanations may be longer, but should have subheadings.

---

# 9. Active Voice

Prefer:

> We design the system around your workflow.

Avoid:

> The system is designed around your workflow by our team.

Active voice is clearer and more confident.

---

# 10. Avoid Empty Adjectives

Use fewer words like:

```text
innovative
revolutionary
cutting-edge
world-class
powerful
seamless
robust
scalable
transformative
next-generation
```

These are acceptable only when supported by specific context.

---

# 11. “Scalable” Rule

Do not say:

> Highly scalable architecture.

Explain what scalability means:

```text
handles increasing traffic
supports additional locations
supports more users
separates workloads
allows independent deployment
```

Only state the characteristics that are actually true.

---

# 12. “Seamless” Rule

Avoid:

> Seamless integration.

Prefer:

> Connect your existing CRM, payment system and internal workflow without forcing the team to work across disconnected processes.

Only describe integrations that are actually supported.

---

# 13. “Innovative” Rule

Do not call something innovative.

Explain what makes it different.

---

# 14. “AI” Rule

Use AI terminology only when AI is actually involved.

Good:

```text
LLM-assisted support
document classification
AI-powered extraction
AI workflow automation
```

Bad:

```text
AI-powered everything
AI-driven digital transformation
AI-first business ecosystem
```

---

# 15. Technical Language

Technical terminology is welcome when it helps explain the solution.

Examples:

```text
API
WebRTC
PostgreSQL
Redis
event-driven workflows
authentication
role-based access
observability
```

But immediately explain why the technology matters when the audience may not know it.

---

# 16. Technology Should Support the Story

Do not write:

> We use React, Node.js, PostgreSQL, Docker and AWS.

as the main selling point.

Instead:

> The platform uses a typed frontend and transactional backend architecture so customer-facing workflows and internal operations can evolve independently.

Then technology can appear as supporting detail.

---

# 17. Business Language

Use business concepts naturally:

```text
operations
workflow
cost
time
visibility
conversion
customer experience
data
growth
risk
efficiency
```

But avoid turning every paragraph into consulting jargon.

---

# 18. Problem-First Writing

Prefer:

> Orders, inventory and customer records live in separate systems.

Then:

> We connect the workflow so the team does not have to enter the same information three times.

This is stronger than:

> We provide integrated enterprise solutions.

---

# 19. Outcome Language

Outcomes should be concrete.

Examples:

```text
less manual entry
faster processing
better visibility
fewer disconnected tools
clearer reporting
simpler operations
```

Measured outcomes require evidence.

---

# 20. Homepage Headline Rules

A homepage headline should:

```text
be short
be distinctive
communicate positioning
avoid clichés
work without animation
```

The existing line:

> WE ENGINEER DIGITAL DOMINANCE.

should be treated as an important existing brand asset.

Do not replace it casually.

---

# 21. Supporting Hero Copy

The supporting statement should clarify what “digital dominance” means in practical terms.

It should connect:

```text
business problem
+
technology
+
execution
```

Avoid repeating the headline in different words.

---

# 22. Headline Hierarchy

Use:

```text
H1 → positioning
H2 → idea/section
H3 → specific item
body → explanation
label → metadata
```

Do not use heading levels purely for visual size.

---

# 23. Eyebrows / Labels

Eyebrows can use concise technical/editorial labels:

```text
01 / CAPABILITIES
02 / SELECTED WORK
03 / PROCESS
04 / COMPANY
```

The existing numbered-navigation language can influence this system.

Do not add labels to every paragraph.

---

# 24. Numbered Language

Numbering should communicate structure.

Good:

```text
01 / DISCOVERY
02 / ARCHITECTURE
03 / BUILD
```

Bad:

```text
01 / AMAZING SERVICE
02 / POWERFUL SOLUTION
03 / FUTURE READY
```

---

# 25. CTA Voice

CTAs should be direct.

Good:

```text
Discuss a project
Talk to an engineer
Explore the work
See how it works
Discuss this problem
Start a conversation
```

Avoid:

```text
Let's Go!
Get Started Now!!!
Unlock Your Future
Transform My Business
Yes, I Want This
```

---

# 26. Primary CTA

One primary CTA should dominate a page.

The exact wording should match the user's intent.

---

# 27. Secondary CTA

Secondary actions should be quieter.

Examples:

```text
View work
Explore services
Read the approach
Meet the team
```

---

# 28. Button Length

Prefer concise labels.

Generally:

```text
2–5 words
```

Longer labels are acceptable when necessary for clarity.

---

# 29. Contact Form Copy

The form should feel conversational.

Good:

> Tell us what you're trying to solve.

Better than:

> Please provide a detailed description of your requirements.

---

# 30. Form Helper Text

Use helper text to reduce uncertainty.

Example:

> A rough description is enough. We can clarify the technical details together.

Do not ask users to already know the solution.

---

# 31. Form Error Copy

Errors should be clear and human.

Good:

> Enter a valid work email.

Bad:

> Invalid input.

---

# 32. Form Success Copy

Do not use fake enthusiasm.

Good:

> Message received. We'll review the problem and get back to you.

Only promise a response timeframe if the company actually guarantees it.

---

# 33. Error Copy

Good:

> Something went wrong while loading this page. Try again or return to the homepage.

Bad:

> Oops! Something awesome broke!

---

# 34. 404 Copy

The 404 page can have personality, but must remain useful.

Example direction:

> This route does not exist.

Then:

```text
Return home
Explore services
View our work
```

---

# 35. Service Page Copy

Each service should begin with the problem it addresses.

Structure:

```text
problem
→
approach
→
deliverables
→
proof
→
CTA
```

---

# 36. Service Naming

Prefer business-oriented service names.

Good:

```text
Custom Software
Business Automation
Web & Mobile Applications
System Integration
AI Automation
```

Avoid creating separate services for every framework.

---

# 37. Case Study Voice

Case studies should sound factual.

Use:

```text
The client needed...
The existing process...
We designed...
The system handles...
The platform integrates...
```

Avoid:

```text
We completely revolutionized...
An incredible journey...
A game-changing transformation...
```

---

# 38. Case Study Technical Detail

Technical detail should answer:

```text
Why was this architecture chosen?
What constraint existed?
What was difficult?
How did the system handle it?
```

Do not dump code snippets unless they genuinely help.

---

# 39. Case Study Metrics

Use exact numbers only when verified.

Good:

> The platform processes X transactions per day.

if documented.

Bad:

> The platform delivered 300% more efficiency.

if nobody measured it.

---

# 40. About Page Voice

The About page should sound personal and grounded.

Explain:

```text
what the company believes
how it works
what it is trying to build
```

Avoid:

> Zytrixon is a leading global technology company dedicated to empowering businesses.

That could describe thousands of companies.

---

# 41. Team Bios

Team bios should be concise and authentic.

Use:

```text
role
expertise
responsibility
relevant experience
```

Avoid exaggerated biographies.

---

# 42. Insight Voice

Insights can be more opinionated than service pages.

They should:

```text
take a position
explain reasoning
show examples
acknowledge limitations
```

Do not confuse confidence with certainty.

---

# 43. Technical Article Style

Technical articles should include:

```text
context
problem
approach
trade-offs
implementation
lessons
```

Avoid tutorials that are merely rewritten documentation unless that is intentionally the purpose.

---

# 44. Business Article Style

Business-oriented articles should connect strategy to systems.

Example topics:

```text
Why adding software can make operations worse
When an ERP should be customized
What to automate first
How disconnected systems create hidden costs
```

---

# 45. SEO Copy

SEO should naturally emerge from useful content.

Do not:

```text
repeat keywords
write awkward headings
hide keywords
create doorway pages
```

---

# 46. Meta Titles

Metadata should be:

```text
specific
descriptive
unique
human-readable
```

Do not use:

> Best Web Development Company | Best Web Development Services | Zytrixon

---

# 47. Meta Descriptions

Describe the actual page.

Do not turn metadata into keyword lists.

---

# 48. Capitalization

Recommended:

```text
sentence case
```

for normal body copy.

Brand/editorial labels may use:

```text
UPPERCASE
```

sparingly.

---

# 49. Exclamation Marks

Use rarely.

A technical company does not need:

```text
We build amazing products!
```

Confidence should come from the statement itself.

---

# 50. Emoji

Do not use emojis in core website copy unless there is a deliberate brand reason.

Zytrixon's current visual language does not require them.

---

# 51. Slashes and Symbols

Editorial symbols such as:

```text
01 /
→
+
```

can be part of the visual system.

Use them consistently.

Do not add random symbols to make ordinary copy look technical.

---

# 52. Jargon Test

After writing a paragraph, remove unnecessary buzzwords.

If the meaning becomes clearer, keep the simpler version.

---

# 53. “Could Another Agency Say This?”

This is a mandatory review question.

If a paragraph could appear on:

```text
web agency A
SaaS agency B
AI agency C
marketing agency D
```

without modification, rewrite it.

---

# 54. Truth Test

For every factual statement ask:

```text
Can we prove this?
```

If no:

```text
remove
qualify
or verify
```

---

# 55. Human Test

Read the copy aloud.

If it sounds like:

```text
a corporate brochure
AI-generated LinkedIn post
marketing template
```

rewrite it.

---

# 56. Specificity Test

Replace abstract nouns with concrete nouns.

Instead of:

> digital transformation

consider:

> replacing spreadsheet-based inventory tracking with a connected business system.

Only when that is actually the work being described.

---

# 57. Banned / Discouraged Phrase Bank

Avoid unless genuinely justified:

```text
cutting-edge
state-of-the-art
world-class
next-generation
revolutionary
game-changing
seamless
robust
scalable solution
innovative solution
digital transformation
unlock potential
unlock growth
empower businesses
drive innovation
future-ready
one-stop solution
end-to-end solution
holistic approach
360-degree solution
leverage technology
synergy
ecosystem
disruptive
redefine
reimagine
```

These words are not forbidden in all contexts, but they require a specific reason.

---

# 58. Preferred Phrase Bank

Use concrete language such as:

```text
build
connect
simplify
map
measure
automate
integrate
replace
reduce
design
ship
test
monitor
improve
operate
```

---

# 59. Brand Vocabulary

Words that fit Zytrixon's identity:

```text
engineering
systems
software
business
workflow
architecture
integration
automation
operations
product
infrastructure
data
process
clarity
execution
```

Use naturally.

---

# 60. Words to Use Carefully

These can become vague if overused:

```text
innovation
transformation
growth
scale
digital
strategy
platform
ecosystem
intelligence
```

Always explain what they mean.

---

# 61. Copy and Design Relationship

Copy should not be written independently of layout.

A headline must survive:

```text
desktop
tablet
mobile
```

without requiring extreme font sizes.

---

# 62. Do Not Design Around Placeholder Copy

Avoid building the final layout around:

```text
Lorem ipsum
AI-generated filler
fake statistics
temporary testimonials
```

Content and design should evolve together.

---

# 63. Localization

If additional languages are introduced later:

```text
content should be translation-ready
text should not be embedded inside images
layout should tolerate longer strings
```

Do not add multilingual infrastructure until it is actually required.

---

# 64. Accessibility and Copy

Copy must remain understandable when visual hierarchy is removed.

A screen-reader user should still receive meaningful:

```text
headings
links
button labels
form instructions
errors
```

---

# 65. Content Density

Not every section needs more text.

Use:

```text
headline
short explanation
structured metadata
visual proof
```

where that communicates the idea better.

---

# 66. Progressive Disclosure

Complex information can be revealed through:

```text
accordions
tabs
details
case-study sections
```

but essential information should not be hidden unnecessarily.

---

# 67. Microcopy

Microcopy includes:

```text
labels
tooltips
form hints
empty states
loading states
errors
success messages
navigation labels
```

It should follow the same voice.

---

# 68. No Fake Personality

Do not force jokes, slang, or casual language into every interaction.

Human does not mean childish.

---

# 69. No Overly Formal Language

Avoid:

> Kindly furnish the requisite information regarding your proposed engagement.

Prefer:

> Tell us what you're trying to solve.

---

# 70. No Artificial Humility

Avoid:

> We are humbled and honored to potentially serve your esteemed organization.

Say what the company actually does.

---

# 71. No Aggressive Sales Language

Avoid:

```text
You need this now.
Don't get left behind.
Your competitors are already doing it.
Act before it's too late.
```

unless a specific, factual context makes such language appropriate.

---

# 72. Conversion Principle

The website should convert through:

```text
clarity
+
proof
+
trust
+
relevance
```

not pressure.

---

# 73. Copy Review Checklist

Before publishing:

```text
[ ] Is it specific?
[ ] Is it true?
[ ] Is it useful?
[ ] Is it understandable?
[ ] Does it sound like Zytrixon?
[ ] Could another agency use it unchanged?
[ ] Are claims verified?
[ ] Are metrics verified?
[ ] Are CTAs clear?
[ ] Does it work on mobile?
[ ] Is it accessible?
```

---

# 74. Final Voice Rule

**Write like people who have actually built systems.**

Do not tell the visitor that Zytrixon is:

```text
innovative
world-class
revolutionary
future-ready
```

Show it through:

```text
the problems described
the systems explained
the engineering decisions
the work presented
the evidence provided
```

---

# 75. Final Principle

**Specific beats impressive. Clear beats clever. Evidence beats hype.**

Zytrixon's copy should make the reader think:

> “These people understand the problem.”

not:

> “These people know a lot of marketing words.”
